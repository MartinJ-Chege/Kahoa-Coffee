Kenyan Coffee Guide

Nyeri, Kirinyaga & Murang'a: Kenya's coffee regions

Where Kenya's most loved coffee grows, why the highlands taste the way they do, and where Kahoa's beans come from.

The short answer: Most of Kenya's best-known coffee grows on the red volcanic slopes around Mount Kenya and the Aberdare Range, in counties like Nyeri, Kirinyaga and Murang'a, at roughly 1,300 to 2,000 meters above sea level.

Why this part of Kenya grows such distinctive coffee

High altitude means cool nights and slow-ripening cherries, which build sweetness and bright acidity. The soils are deep, well-drained red volcanic earth. Rain comes in two seasons, and the main harvest runs roughly from October into December.

RegionAltitude (approx.)Often tastes like
Nyeri1,600 to 2,000 mCitrus acidity and blackcurrant

These are general tendencies. Every farm and every harvest is a little different.

Nyeri

Nyeri sits between Mount Kenya and the Aberdare Range and is one of Kenya's most celebrated coffee counties. Nyeri lots are known for lively, citrusy acidity and a deep blackcurrant sweetness.

Kirinyaga

Kirinyaga lies on the southern slopes of Mount Kenya. Its coffees are often floral and juicy, with berry notes like cranberry and blackcurrant.

Murang'a

Murang'a is on the slopes of the Aberdares. Its coffees tend to be floral and fruity, with berry and citrus notes and a smooth, sweet finish.

How Kenyan coffee is grown and processed

Many Kenyan growers are smallholders who deliver their ripe cherries to a nearby cooperative washing station, often called a factory. There the coffee is pulped, fermented, washed and dried, a method known as washed processing that gives the clean, bright cup Kenya is famous for. Common varieties include SL28 and SL34, prized for flavor, and newer disease-resistant varieties like Ruiru 11 and Batian.

Frequently asked questions

Where does Kenyan coffee come from?

Much of Kenya's famous coffee grows on the volcanic slopes around Mount Kenya and the Aberdare Range, in counties such as Nyeri, Kirinyaga, Murang'a, Kiambu and Embu.

What does Nyeri coffee taste like?

Nyeri coffees are known for bright citrus acidity and blackcurrant sweetness. Individual farms and harvests vary.

What does Kirinyaga coffee taste like?

Kirinyaga coffees are often floral and juicy, with berry notes such as cranberry and blackcurrant.

What is a coffee factory in Kenya?

A factory is a cooperative washing station where many small farmers bring their ripe cherries to be pulped, fermented, washed and dried.

What altitude is Kenyan coffee grown at?

Most of Kenya's specialty coffee is grown at roughly 1,300 to 2,000 meters above sea level, which helps build sweetness and acidity.

Where does Kahoa source its coffee?

Kahoa sources single-origin Kenyan coffee from farms in Nyeri, Kirinyaga and Murang'a, including the founder's family farm in the Kenyan highlands.
